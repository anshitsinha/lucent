How it works

A neural network, hand-wired
into your graphics card.

  1. 01

    Tile

    Your image is cut into 512 px tiles, each padded with 24 px of real neighbours so the seams vanish.

  2. 02

    34 layers

    Real-ESRGAN's compact network runs as 34 hand-written WGSL compute kernels in fp16, 3 TFLOP/s.

  3. 03

    Reshape

    Pixel shuffle to 4×, then Lanczos to your exact size, blended and sharpened.

  4. 04

    Paint

    Tiles stream back while the GPU starts the next one, nearest-to-your-view first.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

Is it really free? What's the catch?

No catch. All the compute happens on your hardware, so there is no server bill to pass on. The app downloads about 6 MB of model weights once (cached afterwards) and that's the only traffic.

Where do my images go?

Nowhere. They're decoded, enhanced and encoded inside the browser tab. There's no backend to send them to. Open your browser's network panel and watch.

Which browsers work?

Anything with WebGPU: Chrome, Edge and Brave on desktop, Safari 26+, and Chrome on Android. Half precision (fp16) is used where the GPU supports it, for about twice the speed.

How big can images get?

Up to 16× the input size and about 120 megapixels of output, the most a browser canvas holds reliably. Above 4×, lucent runs two AI passes.

What model is it?

Real-ESRGAN's compact models (BSD-3-Clause, by Xintao Wang et al.), converted to fp16 and run on WebGPU kernels written for lucent. No ONNX Runtime, no WASM.

Is the added detail "real"?

It's reconstructed: the network predicts plausible texture and edges from what's there. That's what makes it look great, and it's also why you shouldn't use it as forensic evidence.
